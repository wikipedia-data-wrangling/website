# Research

The database was built for a study of Wikipedia's protection policy. Protection lets administrators restrict who may edit a page: only registered accounts, only established ones, or only administrators. It is meant to reduce harm such as vandalism, edit wars and attacks on the subjects of articles. It also excludes people from editing. The study asks what that exclusion does to the people who edit around a protected page, and to the page itself.

## A network question

Protection acts on a network rather than on a single page. Editors work on many pages, and pages are shaped by many editors. When a page is protected, the editors shut out of it may move their work elsewhere, and the editors who remain must coordinate differently. The study therefore observes two networks:

- a **two-mode network** of editors and the pages they edit;
- a **one-mode network** of discussion among a page's editors on its talk page.

## Outcomes

1. **Attachment**: which editors start, continue or stop editing which pages.
2. **Discussion**: who replies to whom on the talk page, and how collaboration is organised.
3. **Quality**: how the quality of the article changes, measured with automated article-quality scores.

## Identification

Pages are not protected at random. They are protected because something has already gone wrong, so comparing protected with unprotected pages confounds the effect of protection with its causes. The study uses Requests for page protection instead. Each request records a page that someone judged to need protection and an administrator's decision on it. Comparing granted with denied requests holds that judgement constant.

Administrators also differ in how readily they grant requests. If which administrator answers a request is as good as random, that leniency can serve as an instrument for protection. Relational event models then describe how editors, pages and discussions evolve around each decision, at the level of individual edits and replies.

## Status

Data collection finished in September 2026, and a first estimation on a sample of the requests followed. The results are not reported on this site.
